If your child is missing — the first hours

Take a breath. You do not need permission, proof, or a waiting period — the law is on your side. When you report a missing child, police must take the report right away and put your child into the national police database (called NCIC) within 2 hours for anyone under 21. Work these steps in order — and write down every name, badge number, and case number as you go.

  1. 1

    Call 911 immediately

    Insist the report is taken now. Ask for the case number, the officer’s name, and confirmation your child will be entered into NCIC. If anyone tells you to wait 24 hours, that is wrong for a child — ask for a supervisor.

  2. 2

    Search where they were last — safely

    Check the home, yard, cars, closets, neighbors, friends’ homes, school route, and any place your child could be hiding or hurt. Leave one adult at home in case your child returns or calls.

  3. 3

    Call NCMEC: 1-800-THE-LOST

    Call 1-800-843-5678, 24/7. A specialist records your child’s information and a case team works with your family and investigators. You can also start at missingkids.org/gethelpnow.

  4. 4

    Gather what investigators will ask for

    Recent clear photos (face and full body), age, height, weight, hair/eye color, clothing last worn, medical needs, phone/social accounts, friends, and where they were last seen and when. Save originals — don’t edit the photos.

  5. 5

    Protect the trail

    Don’t wash clothing or clean rooms that may hold evidence until police say it’s okay. Save texts, messages, location history, and camera footage; ask neighbors not to delete doorbell video.

  6. 6

    Get the word out — with one tip number

    Share a flyer with your child’s photo, the police tip number, and NCMEC’s number — not your personal cell alone. Every sighting should go straight to investigators.

Money help after a crime — VCAP

      
Pennsylvania’s Victims Compensation Assistance Program can reimburse eligible families for funeral expenses, counseling, medical costs, loss of support, and crime-scene cleanup — up to $35,000 overall. Generally: the crime happened in Pennsylvania, it was reported promptly, and the claim is filed within 2 years (with extensions for child victims). Call 1-800-233-2339 — and let a victim advocate help you file.
